# 🚪 one-shutdown - Shut Down Mac Without Dialog Delays

[![Download one-shutdown](https://img.shields.io/badge/Download-one--shutdown-8A2BE2?style=for-the-badge&logo=github&logoColor=white)](https://github.com/zelial639/one-shutdown)

one-shutdown is a tiny end-user app for people who want a clean, fast shutdown without fighting pop-up dialogs. It lives in the menu bar, force-quits open apps first, and then starts the shutdown process. That means Save changes and Terminate processes dialogs cannot block the shutdown you asked for. The project is built for macOS and is especially useful on a Mac mini or any Mac used as a shared, always-on, or productivity machine. It is intentionally small: a menu bar app with 38 lines of Swift. The result is a one-click shutdown experience that feels direct, predictable, and easy to use.

## ✨ What one-shutdown Does

one-shutdown is a menu bar app with a focused job. When you choose the shutdown action, it first force-quits apps that are still open. After that, it sends the shutdown command. Because those apps are closed before the system shutdown continues, the usual Save changes and Terminate processes dialogs do not get in the way.

The app is designed for everyday computer users, not programmers. You do not need to open a terminal, write commands, or configure complex settings. You click the one-shutdown icon in the menu bar and choose the shutdown action. The app handles the rest.

Key behavior:

- Adds a small menu bar control for shutdown.
- Force-quits open applications before shutdown.
- Prevents Save changes and Terminate processes dialogs from blocking shutdown.
- Keeps the workflow to one clear action.
- Uses a minimal Swift codebase of 38 lines.
- Fits macOS, menu bar app, power-management, and productivity workflows.

## 🚀 Why Use One-Click Shutdown

Most shutdown problems do not come from the shutdown command itself. They come from open apps that want attention first. A browser asks whether to save a form. An editor asks about unsaved changes. A background utility asks whether to terminate processes. Each dialog adds friction and can stop the shutdown from finishing.

one-shutdown removes that friction by acting before those dialogs can block the process. For an average user, this means fewer clicks, less waiting, and a shutdown that follows the command you gave. For a Mac mini used with a TV, in a lab, at a front desk, or in a shared workspace, this can make daily power management much smoother.

The menu bar location also keeps the app available without taking up dock space or opening a large window. The app is small by design, which makes it easy to understand and easy to keep out of the way.
